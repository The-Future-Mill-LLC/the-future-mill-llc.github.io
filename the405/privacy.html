The 405: Privacy Policy

Effective October 21, 2026

The 405 is a driving and traffic-jam puzzle game made by The Future Mill LLC, doing business as LOVE405 ("we", "us"). This page explains what the game collects, what it doesn't, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, email, phone number, photos, contacts or location.
  • The game asks your birth year once, so younger players only get ads made for kids. The answer stays on your iPhone.
  • We collect anonymous gameplay numbers (like which levels get played and finished) so we can make the game better.
  • The free version shows ads from Google. If you tap "Allow" when your iPhone asks about tracking, those ads can be personalised. If you tap "Ask App Not to Track", they won't be.
  • We don't sell personal information.

What we don't collect

The 405 does not ask for or collect your name, email address, phone number, street address, contacts, photos, microphone, camera or your device's location. The weather in the game comes from Apple Weather for a fixed spot in Los Angeles, not from where you are.

Your birth year

The first time you open The 405 it asks what year you were born. We use the answer only on your iPhone, to decide which kind of ads you see (see below). It is saved on your iPhone, is never sent to us, Google or anyone else, and is deleted if you delete the game.

Gameplay analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To see how the game is played, we use Google Analytics for Firebase. It records game events such as levels started and finished, crashes into traffic jams, puzzle results, difficulty settings, speed runs, and shop and ad screens being shown. Along with those events Google receives:

  • a random ID created for this installation of the game (it isn't your name or Apple ID, and it changes if you delete and reinstall the game),
  • basic device details such as the iPhone model, iOS version, app version and language,
  • your approximate region (country and city level), which Google works out from your internet connection.

We have set Firebase so this data is not used for advertising or ad personalisation. We use it only as totals and trends, and we don't try to identify anyone. We also use Firebase Remote Config, which lets us adjust settings such as how often ads appear without releasing an update.

Ads (Google AdMob)

The free version of The 405 shows a full-screen ad after every few finished levels, and you can choose to watch an ad to earn a token. These ads come from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, and to prevent ad fraud, Google receives your IP address, device details, and information about the ads you see and tap.

What happens next depends on the birth year you gave:

  • Under 13: Google is told the player is a child, so ads are chosen under its child-directed rules: general-audience (G-rated) ads only, never personalised, and no advertising identifier. The tracking question and privacy pop-ups are never shown.
  • 13 to 15: ads are never personalised and are limited to teen-appropriate content. The tracking question and privacy pop-ups are never shown.
  • 16 and over: your iPhone asks whether The 405 may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites, as described next.

For players 16 and over:

  • If you tap "Allow", Google may also use your device's advertising identifier to show ads based on your interests.
  • If you tap "Ask App Not to Track", the advertising identifier isn't shared and ads are not personalised.

You can change your answer at any time in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Tracking. Where the law requires it (for example in the UK, the European Union and some US states), Google will also show a consent screen, and an "Ad privacy choices" link in the game's shop lets you change those choices later.

If you buy "Ad-Free" or "405 Live", the game stops showing these ads.

405 Live traffic

If you own 405 Live and play in real-time mode, the game downloads a small file of current 405 traffic from our website (hosted by GitHub) every few minutes. Like any website, GitHub sees your internet address when the file is downloaded; the game sends nothing else. The traffic itself comes from TomTom, which never receives anything from your iPhone.

Purchases, Game Center and crash reports (Apple)

  • Purchases are handled entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details; Apple only tells the game which items you own.
  • Game Center leaderboards are run by Apple. If you sign in to Game Center, your Game Center nickname and your speed run times appear on the leaderboards for other players to see. You can manage this in your iPhone's Game Center settings.
  • Crash and performance reports are shared with us by Apple only if you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings. They don't identify you.

Children

The 405 is designed to be fun for all ages. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children. Players who give a birth year under 13 only get child-directed, non-personalised ads and never see the tracking question, and their gameplay analytics stay anonymous like everyone else's. If a parent sets up the game for a child, please enter the child's birth year. In-app purchases go through Apple's own purchase approval, including Ask to Buy for Family Sharing. If you believe we have collected personal information from a child, contact us and we will delete it.

How long data is kept

Your progress, stars and tokens are saved only on your iPhone, and deleting the game deletes them. Gameplay analytics are kept by Google for a limited time under our account's retention settings and are then deleted automatically. Google's own retention of ad data is described in Google's policies below.

Your choices

  • Answer "Ask App Not to Track", or change it later in Settings, to stop personalised ads.
  • Buy "Ad-Free" to stop ads altogether.
  • Use "Ad privacy choices" in the shop, where shown, to change your ad consent.
  • Delete the game to remove everything stored on your device, including your birth year, and reset its random analytics ID.
  • Email us to ask about, or ask us to delete, any data connected to your use of the game. Depending on where you live (for example California, the UK or the EU), you may have legal rights to access or delete your data, and we will honour them.
